## Enforcement cases (for scam / red-flag content)
- **Red Rock Secured (2023):** sued by the CFTC, SEC, California and Hawaii. It took $61.8M from about 950 customers, with average markups of 100–130% against disclosed markups of 4–29%. The money came from IRAs, 401(k)s and TSPs.
  - Source: https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8704-23
- **Safeguard Metals:** $68M; markups averaging 50% more than disclosed; settled with the CFTC in October 2023. NY AG case "defrauding seniors".
- **Goldline (2012):** Santa Monica judgment requiring up to $4.5M in refunds plus an $800k fund; coin markups above 55%.
- **Metals.com:** Massachusetts action; one couple was charged a 54% markup.
- **CFTC/FINRA/NASAA joint advisory** "10 Things to Ask Before Buying Physical Gold, Silver, or Other Metals" (Mar 20, 2024):
  - https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/Metals10Things.html

## Fees insight
- Annual fees cluster at **$175–$300/yr, flat** (not a percentage).
- The real cost is the **spread or markup** on the metal itself:
  - Roughly 5–10% on common bullion.
  - 30–130% on "premium", proof or numismatic coins in the enforcement cases.
  - Few dealers publish it.
- **Buyback spread:** the gap between the price you pay and the price you can sell back at.

## Rules and figures
- **IRC 408(m):** collectibles held in an IRA count as distributions. There is an exception for certain US-minted coins (e.g. the American Gold Eagle, 91.67% gold) and for bullion that meets fineness standards and is held by a trustee.
- **Fineness:**
  - Gold .995 (Gold Eagle excepted)
  - Silver .999
  - Platinum and palladium .9995 [K]
- **Commonly eligible:**
  - American Gold Eagle and Gold Buffalo
  - Canadian Maple Leaf
  - Austrian Philharmonic
  - American Silver Eagle
  - Bars from accredited refiners [K]
- **Not eligible:** Krugerrands, most proof or numismatic "rare" coins, and collectibles.
- **Storage:** an IRS-approved non-bank trustee or custodian must hold the metal, in an approved depository.
  - Home storage through an "IRA LLC" was treated as a taxable distribution in *McNulty v. Commissioner*, 157 T.C. No. 10 (2021).
- **2026 limits** (IRS Notice 2025-67):
  - IRA: $7,500, plus $1,100 catch-up at 50+ (total $8,600).
  - 401(k): $24,500, plus $8,000 catch-up, or $11,250 at ages 60–63.
- **Rollovers:**
  - A direct trustee-to-trustee transfer is not taxed and has no limit on how often it can be done.
  - An indirect 60-day rollover is limited to once per 12 months for IRA-to-IRA moves. When a 401(k) pays the money to you, it withholds 20%.
- **RMDs:**
  - Start at age 73 (SECURE 2.0; rises to 75 in 2033).
  - Can be taken in cash, or "in kind" by distributing the metal (taxed at fair market value).
  - Roth IRAs have no RMDs for the owner.
- **TSP:** in-service withdrawal at 59½, or after separation from service.
